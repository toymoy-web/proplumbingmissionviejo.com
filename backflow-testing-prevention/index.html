Backflow Testing and Prevention in Mission Viejo

Backflow is the reversal of water flow, which can pull contaminated water from an irrigation system, a fire line or a pool fill back into the drinking water supply. A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that blocks that reversal, and backflow testing confirms it still works.

California's cross-connection rules require certain assemblies to be tested at least once a year, and your water supplier sets the schedule. In Mission Viejo that supplier is Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District or El Toro Water District, depending on your street.

Start Here

I Got a Notice From My Water District to Test My Backflow Device

This is the usual reason people call. Districts mail an annual reminder to every property with a registered assembly. The letter lists the device location and a deadline, and it tells you to have a certified tester inspect it and send the result.

Missing the deadline can bring follow-up notices and, in some cases, the district can turn off service until the test is filed. Booking early keeps the process routine.

The tester should be certified and recognized by your district. Ask for the certification number and confirm that your district accepts it before the visit.

What to Do Before the Backflow Tester Arrives

  • Find the district notice and note the account number and the device location it lists.
  • Clear shrubs, mulch and stored items so the tester can reach the assembly and its test ports.
  • Expect a short water shutoff to the irrigation line or the building, and tell tenants or staff in advance.
  • Look over the device for cracks, drips and sun damage, and mention anything you find.
  • Keep the passing report. Your district may ask for it again, and a lender or insurer sometimes does too.

The Process

How a Backflow Test Goes

  1. 1

    Locate

    The tester finds the assembly and confirms its type and serial number.

  2. 2

    Test

    Pressure gauges check each check valve and relief valve for leakage.

  3. 3

    Repair

    A failed device gets new seals, springs or check valves, then is retested.

  4. 4

    Report

    The result is filed with the district on the required form.

Local Context

Why Cross-Connection Control Matters Here

Mission Viejo irrigates over 500 acres of parks, medians, slopes and open space with reclaimed water, according to the City of Mission Viejo. Recycled and potable systems must stay completely separate, and backflow assemblies protect that separation.

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires backflow protection at specific points. The state's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook, which replaced the old Title 17 sections, then sets the program rules. Santa Margarita Water District's plan under that handbook took effect on July 1, 2025, and each district enforces its own schedule.

Homes with irrigation systems often have a pressure vacuum breaker above ground near the lawn. Hot sun, freezing and impacts damage them, so a visual check helps even before the annual test.
