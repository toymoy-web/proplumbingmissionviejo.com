Shower and Bathtub Installation in Mission Viejo

Shower and bathtub installation covers the plumbing and waterproofing behind a finished bathroom: the supply valve, the drain, the shower pan or tub, the wall membrane and the connections. Tile and fixtures are the visible part, but the work behind the wall decides whether the shower leaks in five years.

Many Mission Viejo homes date from 1966 to the 1990s, and their original tubs, valves and drains are now at the age where homeowners remodel. Replacing a tub or converting it to a walk-in shower is among the most common bathroom projects in houses of that era.

Code Requirements

What the Plumbing Code Requires Behind the Tile

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), requires shower valves to be pressure balance or thermostatic types that guard against scalding when other fixtures run. A shower floor must slope to a drain and be waterproof, and the finished pan is typically tested before it is covered.

California also limits showerheads to 1.8 gallons per minute, so a new head will be a low-flow model. A permit and inspection normally cover bathroom plumbing alterations, and the plumber should pull it.

Condominiums and attached homes add a layer. Association rules may require approval, water shutoff notice to neighbors and a licensed contractor on site. Palmia and Casta del Sol are both gated associations, so ask your HOA for its remodel rules first.

The Process

How a Shower or Tub Project Goes

  1. 1

    Plan

    The plumber measures, checks the drain, valve and floor, and discusses the layout.

  2. 2

    Rough-in

    The valve and drain are set, the pan or tub is installed and the plumbing is tested.

  3. 3

    Inspect

    The city inspects the rough plumbing before walls are closed and waterproofed.

  4. 4

    Trim out

    After tile, the valve trim, showerhead and drain are installed and the system is tested.

Questions to Settle Before You Order Anything

  • Where will the drain sit, and does the slab or subfloor allow it to move?
  • Do you want a curb, a low threshold or a fully curbless shower?
  • Which valve will you use, and is the model compatible with the trim you picked?
  • Who is responsible for waterproofing, and is a flood test part of the job?
  • Will you need HOA approval, a permit or neighbor notice before work starts?
