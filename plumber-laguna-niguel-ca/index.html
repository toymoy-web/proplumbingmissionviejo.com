Laguna Niguel Plumbing Starts With the Year Your Home Was Built

Laguna Niguel was one of California's first master-planned communities. The Laguna Niguel Corporation launched its plan in 1959, land sales began in 1961 in Monarch Bay and Laguna Terrace, and the city incorporated on December 1, 1989. That sequence puts pipes of very different ages under one city, so the age of your house tells you what to expect.

The earliest tracts date to the early 1960s. Homes that old have seen more than 60 years of water, soil movement and remodeling, and original supply valves, drain lines and fixture connections are the usual failures.

Marina Hills added about 1,500 homes from the late 1980s into the mid-1990s, and Niguel Summit's Spanish Revival subdivisions also date from the 1980s. Those houses are now roughly 30 to 40 years old, the stretch when copper starts to pinhole and a second water heater is due.

Water Supply

Moulton Niguel Water District and Your Hillside Pressure

Moulton Niguel Water District serves all of Laguna Niguel. Ranchers formed it in 1960 to import Colorado River water because the area had too little local supply. That imported water carries dissolved minerals, which settle as scale in heaters, aerators and shower valves.

The city covers just under 15 square miles of the San Joaquin Hills, and elevation changes street pressure. A home low on a slope can see pressure above 80 psi, the level at which the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) calls for a regulator, and high pressure is a quiet cause of burst fittings and dripping valves. A failed regulator is worth checking before a repipe is quoted.

If your meter moves with every fixture off, call the district to verify the read, then call us to find the leak.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, so a pressure-stressed fitting on a hillside street at 2 a.m. gets the same response as a weekday call.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Laguna Niguel

Water spreads fast across a slab, and many older Laguna Niguel tract homes have finished floors over it. These four steps limit the damage while the plumber is on the way.

1

Close the main valve

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. It is usually at the front wall or in the garage near the water heater. The valve in the meter box belongs to Moulton Niguel Water District, so use the one on your side.

2

Shut off nearby breakers

If water is near outlets, the garage panel or the water heater, switch off that breaker before you step in. Never stand in water to reach a panel.

3

Clear the area

Lift rugs, electronics and cardboard off wet floors and open the cabinet doors under the leaking fixture. Tile over a slab holds water longer than most people expect.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call with your street, the nearest cross street and what you see. Tell dispatch if the meter keeps spinning after the main is closed.
