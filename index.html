Emergency Plumbing

24/7 Emergency Plumber Service in Mission Viejo

An emergency plumber is a contractor who answers at any hour and goes to jobs where water, sewage, or gas is already causing damage. Our 24/7 line serves homes in Mission Viejo ZIP codes 92691 and 92692, and the call goes straight to dispatch with no form to fill out first.

Most emergency calls here begin the same way. Water is somewhere it should not be, and it spreads through drywall, flooring and cabinet bases by the hour, so the order of operations matters. Close the main shutoff, then call.

In many tract homes the main shutoff is a lever or round handle near the front of the house or in the meter box at the curb. The steps in our burst pipe repair guide cover what to do while you wait, and the checklist further down this page covers the rest.

Do This Before the Plumber Arrives

  • Close the main water shutoff. If it will not turn, shut the valve at the meter box.
  • Turn off the water heater: set a gas unit to pilot or off, and switch off the breaker on an electric one.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything, since your insurance claim will ask for it.
  • Lift rugs, electronics and cabinet contents out of the wet area.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or SoCalGas at 800-427-2200 from outside.

Local Housing

What Mission Viejo's Housing Eras Mean for Your Pipes

Mission Viejo was planned as one community. The first families moved into the Deane Homes tracts in 1966. Lake Mission Viejo, a private 124-acre lake, was dedicated in 1978. The city incorporated on March 31, 1988, according to the City of Mission Viejo community profile. That span of more than two decades means several generations of plumbing sit inside one city limit, and each ages differently.

Casta del Sol alone, a gated 55+ community, has 1,927 mostly single-story homes built between 1972 and 1987. Copper supply lines and drain pipes that old are where pinhole leaks and slow, root-fed stoppages can start. If your home dates from that stretch, a leak detection visit before anyone opens a wall usually costs less than guessing at the wrong spot.

Palmia, with 901 homes built from 1988 to 1999, falls in a newer window. A house from that decade has usually had its original water heater replaced at least once, so the question is more often when the current tank reaches the 8 to 12 year range. That is the point to price a planned water heater replacement instead of waiting for a leak on a Sunday night.

The layout of the city adds another variable. Mission Viejo's original plan put roads in the valleys and houses on the hills, so homes in Pacific Hills in the north sit differently from those in Casta del Sol in the south or Painted Trails in the east, which was developed in the early 2000s.

Crown Valley Parkway, Marguerite Parkway, Oso Parkway and Alicia Parkway are the main routes dispatch uses. Landmarks such as Saddleback College, Mission Hospital and The Shops at Mission Viejo make quick reference points when you give directions.

Lake Mission Viejo opened to association members on June 3, 1978, and the Mission Viejo Mall followed in 1979. Homes within a short drive of either share the same city water supply as the older tracts, so the first question on any call is which district appears on your bill. Need a pump for a lower level or a garage that floods in storms? See sump pump repair.

Water Supply

Three Water Districts, One City

The City of Mission Viejo lists three suppliers: Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District, and El Toro Water District. Which one bills your home depends on the street, and your water bill names it. That matters for two common calls.

If the meter keeps turning with every fixture off, the leak is somewhere between the meter and the house, and the line itself may be the problem. A water service line repair is a different job from a leak inside the walls, and the district handles everything on its side of the meter. Pressure complaints and high-bill questions also start with a call to your own district.
