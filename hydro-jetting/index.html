Hydro Jetting

Hydro Jetting for Mission Viejo Sewer and Drain Lines

Hydro jetting is the cleaning of a drain or sewer line with a high-pressure stream of water fired from a nozzle on a flexible hose. The nozzle sprays backward and forward, scouring the inside wall of the pipe instead of punching a hole through the clog like a cable does.

That difference matters for lines that keep clogging. A cable opens a channel and leaves a ring of grease, scale or root hair on the wall, which catches the next load of debris within weeks. A jet removes it.

Start Here

We Snaked It and It Clogged Again a Few Weeks Later

This is the most common reason people call about jetting. A plumber clears the line, the water drains for a month or two, and then the same backup returns. The clog was never the problem. The coating on the pipe wall was.

Another version is the cable that gets stuck. A snake that stalls at 60 feet usually hit a mass of roots, a joint offset, or a heavy grease plug. A camera shows which one it is, and the answer decides whether jetting or a repair is next.

Roots are the third pattern. Roots enter at joints and grow into a hair-like mass that traps everything behind it. Jetting with a cutting nozzle clears the roots, but it does not close the joint, so root clogs return unless the pipe is repaired.

Honest Limits

When Hydro Jetting Is Not the Right Tool

Jetting a cracked, collapsed or heavily corroded pipe can make the damage worse and push water into the surrounding soil. That is why the line is inspected with a camera before a jet goes in.

If the camera finds a break, an offset joint or a low spot, the answer is a repair. Our pages on sewer line repair and sewer camera inspection cover what comes next.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which the California Plumbing Code is built on, requires accessible cleanouts on building drains, and that access is what lets a jetter in without pulling fixtures. Jetting also does not fix a single slow fixture. For that, drain cleaning with a cable is faster and costs less.

The Process

How a Jetting Visit Goes

  1. 1

    Camera look

    A camera runs the line to find the clog and check the pipe's condition.

  2. 2

    Access

    The jetter is connected at the cleanout or an opened access point.

  3. 3

    Jet

    The nozzle is fed through the line while the stream cuts roots and strips grease.

  4. 4

    Verify

    A second camera pass confirms the wall is clean and the line flows freely.
