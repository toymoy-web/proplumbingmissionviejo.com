Replacement

Water Heater Replacement in Mission Viejo

Water heater replacement means removing a worn tank or tankless unit and installing a new one, including the gas or electric connection, venting, shutoff valves, relief valve and discharge line. Most units are replaced once they pass 10 years or start to leak.

The city's housing eras set the timeline. A heater in a home built in Casta del Sol between 1972 and 1987 has almost certainly been replaced at least once by now. Homes in Palmia, built from 1988 to 1999, are in the same position, since an original tank from that decade would be more than 25 years past a normal life of 8 to 12 years.

That makes the real question timing. Replacing a 10-year-old tank on a weekday costs less than replacing a failed one at 9 p.m., and it lets you choose the unit instead of taking what is available.

Emergency Service

Emergency Replacement When the Tank Has Failed

A tank that is leaking from the body, flooding the garage or closet, or has failed with no hot water is an emergency replacement. Close the cold water valve above the heater, switch off the gas control or the breaker, and call the 24/7 line.

A tank that leaks from its shell cannot be repaired. Tell dispatch the size, fuel type and location of the heater so the right unit is priced and ready, then we set it, strap it and haul the old one away.

When to Replace

Signs You Should Replace Instead of Repair

The tank itself leaks

Water at the base that comes from the tank body means the steel has corroded through. Fittings can be fixed. The tank cannot.

The unit is past 10 years

A tank that old fails more often, and a repair often buys only a few more months.

Rust-colored hot water

Rust from the hot tap only, not the cold, suggests the inside of the tank is corroding.

It cannot keep up

Running out of hot water mid-shower can mean a failed element or burner, or a unit that is too small for the household.

California Requirements

What the Code Adds to a Replacement in California

California requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake movement, and a replacement should include proper strapping. Homeowners often discover a loose or missing strap only when the old unit comes out.

Replacement also needs a plumbing permit and inspection, which a contractor normally pulls through the City of Mission Viejo. Gas units sold in the South Coast air district must meet low-NOx limits, and garage installations carry extra clearance and vehicle-protection rules.

A gas unit needs a correctly sized supply line and shutoff, and a switch from electric to gas means new piping, which our gas line installation and repair page explains. Many homes also need a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, and a relief valve discharge line piped to a safe spot. These small parts are where a low quote can hide extra charges, so ask what the price includes.

Heat Pump Option

Is a Heat Pump Water Heater Worth Considering

A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank instead of generating it, so it uses far less electricity than a standard electric unit. It needs open air space around it, a condensate drain, and a 240-volt circuit.

State and utility programs have offered rebates on these units, but amounts and eligibility change often. Ask for current figures before you buy, and ask if your panel can handle the new circuit.

Before the Swap

Sizing, Location and Venting Decide the Job

Sizing starts with the household. A 40-gallon tank typically serves two to three people, a 50-gallon tank suits a family of four, and a larger home with several bathrooms may need 75 gallons or a tankless unit. The first-hour rating on the unit's label says more about real hot water supply than tank size alone.

Location changes the work. A heater in the garage is the simplest install. One in an attic or closet needs a drain pan with a drain line, and the relief valve discharge pipe has to end where code allows. Gas units need correct venting and combustion air, and a switch from tank to tankless can call for a larger gas line, as the gas line page explains.

Tell dispatch your heater's age, fuel type and where it sits. That lets the plumber bring the right strap kit, expansion tank and fittings on the first trip. If you are weighing a wall-mounted unit, tankless service covers what the upgrade involves.

Warranty terms vary by model, so ask for them in writing. The questions that matter are how long the tank and parts are covered, whether labor is included and who handles a claim. A heater that arrives with a clear paper trail is easier to defend three years from now.

Water Quality

Hard Water and What It Does to a New Heater

Water supplied to south Orange County is imported and carries dissolved minerals, and Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 15 grains per gallon. Those minerals settle as scale on elements and tank bottoms, which shortens heater life and makes a tank rumble as it heats. Your district's annual water quality report lists the hardness for your street.

A yearly tank flush and an anode rod check every few years slow the damage. A water softener reduces scale at the source, and a tankless owner should book a yearly tankless descaling.
