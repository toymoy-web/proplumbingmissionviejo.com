Toilet Service

Toilet Repair and Installation in Mission Viejo

Toilet repair covers the parts inside the tank, the seal at the floor, the supply line and the flush path, while installation means setting a new toilet on a clean flange with a fresh seal. Most calls fall into one of four groups: a toilet that runs, one that leaks, one that will not flush, and one that rocks.

A running toilet is the most common and the most wasteful. A worn flapper or a stuck fill valve can leak water into the bowl all day, and the water bill is where it shows up. If your district bill has crept up with no change in habits, check the toilets first.

Emergency Service

Emergency Toilet Overflow and Backup Calls

A toilet that overflows, floods the floor or gurgles while other drains slow down is an emergency. Turn the shutoff valve behind the bowl clockwise to stop the refill, and stop using every fixture if more than one drain is slow.

Call the 24/7 line and say whether the water is clean or dirty and whether other fixtures are affected. A single clogged toilet is a quick fix. A backup that reaches the shower or a floor drain points to the main line and needs a different visit.

Symptoms

What Your Toilet Is Telling You

It runs or hisses

A flapper that no longer seals, or a fill valve that will not shut off, lets water trickle into the bowl.

Water around the base

A failed wax ring or a loose bolt lets water escape at the floor. It can also come from a sweating tank.

Weak or slow flush

Clogged rim jets, a low water level, or a partial blockage in the trap.

The toilet rocks

A loose flange or uneven floor is breaking the seal. Rocking can rot the subfloor if it is ignored.

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color reaches the bowl without flushing, the flapper leaks.
  • Look for damp spots or soft flooring around the base, and note any stain on the ceiling below if it is a second-floor bathroom.
  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet if water will not stop.
  • Do not keep plunging an overflowing toilet. Stop, and let the plumber open the line.

Water Use

Why Old Toilets Cost You on the Water Bill

California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush. A toilet made before the 1994 federal standard can use 3.5 gallons or more, which is nearly three times as much water per flush. Mission Viejo's first homes date from 1966, so some original toilets may still be in service in older, lightly updated houses.

Local water districts have offered rebates on high-efficiency toilets in the past, and programs change. Ask your district before you buy, since a rebate can change which model makes sense.

Flush Problems

Weak Flush, Slow Refill and Phantom Flushing

A weak flush with a normal tank usually means the small holes under the rim are clogged with mineral scale. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 15 grains per gallon, so scale is common here. A toilet that refills slowly may have a partly closed supply valve or a worn fill valve.

A toilet that refills by itself every few minutes has a flapper leaking into the bowl. It is the same fault behind most running toilets, and a leaking toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day without any sound.

At the Base

Wobbly Toilets, Leaks at the Base and Wax Rings

A toilet that rocks has loose bolts or a damaged flange. Water seeping from the base points to a failed wax ring or a cracked flange. Do not keep tightening bolts against a cracked bowl, since porcelain breaks without warning.

A base leak is worth fixing quickly because the water runs into the subfloor, where it rots wood and stains the ceiling below. If the floor feels soft near the toilet, say so on the phone.

Replacing It

Choosing a Replacement Toilet

California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush, so the choice is about fit and comfort. Measure the rough-in, the distance from the wall to the flange bolts, which is 12 inches on most homes. Decide between a round and an elongated bowl, and between standard and comfort height.

A clog that keeps returning is a drain question, not a toilet question, and drain cleaning finds out whether the line behind it is the problem.

Install Day

What Is Replaced Along With the Toilet

A new toilet normally comes with a new wax ring or foam gasket, closet bolts and a fresh supply line. The shutoff valve under the tank is worth replacing too, since old valves often seize or weep once they are disturbed. The old toilet is removed and the flange is checked for damage before the new one is set.

A toilet is set level on the flange and bolted down evenly so the porcelain is not stressed. Caulking the base is a matter of taste, but a leak test with a full tank and a few flushes closes the job.

Related Services

When the Toilet Problem Is Bigger Than the Toilet

A toilet that gurgles or backs up along with other drains points to the main line, not the fixture. In that case, drain cleaning is the right call, and a camera check shows whether roots or a break are behind it.

If a damp spot keeps returning around the base after a reseal, hidden supply leaks may be involved, and leak detection can trace them. A new toilet also pairs well with a shower or bathtub installation in the same remodel, and with a faucet and sink repair visit while the plumber is already in the bathroom.
