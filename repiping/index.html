Repiping

Whole-Home Repiping in Mission Viejo

Repiping is the replacement of a home's interior water supply lines with new pipe. It covers the lines inside the house, from the point where water enters at the meter connection to every sink, toilet, tub and appliance. The sewer and drain lines are separate and are not part of a repipe.

That makes repiping different from water line repair and replacement, which covers the buried line from the street or meter to the house. Both can be needed on the same property, and a plumber can price them together.

Local Housing

Why Mission Viejo's Housing Eras Matter for Repiping

The first homes were sold in 1966, and Casta del Sol added 1,927 homes between 1972 and 1987. The copper supply piping in those houses is now about 40 to 60 years old, which is the range where pinhole leaks begin to multiply.

Palmia's 901 homes from 1988 to 1999 are newer, and most still have serviceable pipe. Even so, a house of that age can develop pinholes if water chemistry or flow has worn the copper from the inside. Waiting for a pattern before spending money is sound, as long as you track each leak and its date.

Warning Signs

Signs a Repipe May Be Due

Repeated pinhole leaks

Two or more leaks in a year suggest the pipe wall is thinning all over.

Green or blue stains

Mineral crust or stains on copper show corrosion on the outside.

Discolored water

Brown or blue-green water from the tap can mean pipe corrosion.

Falling pressure

Interior buildup narrows the pipe and cuts flow, especially upstairs.

What to Expect

Living Through a Repipe

A repipe means the water is off for part of each working day, and holes are cut in drywall to reach the old lines. Plumbers use the attic and wall cavities when possible to keep the openings small and to run lines where they can be hidden.

Patching and painting the drywall is a separate trade. Ask if your quote includes it, because some repipe quotes cover only the plumbing, and the drywall work can add real cost.

Work must follow the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the city inspects the finished system. Your plumber pulls the permit.
