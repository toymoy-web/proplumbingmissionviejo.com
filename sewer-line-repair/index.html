Emergency Sewer Line Repair When Sewage Comes Back Up

Sewer line repair fixes a damaged section of the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer main, called the lateral. Damage takes several forms: a crack, a joint pulled apart by roots, a low spot that holds water, or a section that has collapsed.

A sewage backup is a health hazard as well as a repair. Wastewater carries bacteria, so it should be kept off skin and out of living space. Our 24/7 line sends a plumber to clear the line first and then run a camera to find what went wrong.

If Sewage Is Backing Up Now

  • Stop using every sink, tub, toilet and washing machine in the house.
  • Keep children and pets away from the affected area.
  • Wear rubber gloves if you must touch anything, and wash your hands afterward.
  • Open windows if there is a strong odor, and avoid running fans that spread airborne spray.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup for your insurer.
Warning Signs

Signs of a Damaged Sewer Line

Repeat backups

Clearing the line fixes it for a while, then the same fixtures back up again.

Gurgling and slow drains

Several fixtures acting up together points to the lateral, not any single trap.

Sewer smell outside

An odor near the cleanout or in the yard can mean a leak in the buried pipe.

Soft, lush, or sunken ground

Wastewater leaking into soil can green up a patch of lawn or settle the ground above it.

Local Knowledge

Mission Viejo's Trees, Tracts and Who Owns the Pipe

The first Mission Viejo tracts date from 1966, and the planting that followed has produced the mature trees the city is known for. Roots enter sewer laterals at joints, so lines from the 1960s and 1970s are the likeliest to show root damage on camera.

In general, the district owns the main in the street and the homeowner owns the lateral to the house, though Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District each set their own boundary. A camera run shows which side the damage is on, which tells you who pays.

Repairs must meet the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so approved pipe and fittings go in. Work in the street or sidewalk normally needs a city permit, and a plumber should pull it. Ask who pulls it before you sign.
