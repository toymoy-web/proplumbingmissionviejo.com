Water and Sewer

Who Handles Your Water and Sewer in San Juan Capistrano

San Juan Capistrano is unusual in south Orange County. For decades the city ran its own water and sewer utility instead of a district, and in 2019 the council chose Santa Margarita Water District to take over those services. Moulton Niguel Water District and South Coast Water District serve other parts of the area. Santa Margarita Water District now publishes a separate water quality report for San Juan Capistrano, a sign the transition is well along.

That history changes what you should do about a leak or a backup. Check your most recent bill. The name on it tells you which agency to call about the meter and the main. Your responsibility as a homeowner usually begins at the meter for water and at the connection to the main for sewer.

Santa Margarita Water District cited investment in San Juan's aging water and sewer infrastructure when it was chosen. For homeowners, that points to older pipe in the street and often older pipe on private lots too.

Local History

From the Mission to Modern Neighborhoods

Mission San Juan Capistrano was founded in 1776, and the Los Rios Historic District beside it includes 31 historic structures along a street that runs next to Trabuco Creek. Adobes there date to the 1790s, and many small homes were built between 1887 and 1910.

Any repair or replacement must meet the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Historic buildings and old lots mean unusual conditions: shallow or misrouted lines, mixed pipe materials, and work that may need city review. Modern neighborhoods around them were built decades later, so a single street can hold plumbing from three or four eras.

The city also built a separate non-potable water system in 1989 to use recycled water for irrigation. Properties tied to it need backflow protection that is tested every year, which is covered on our backflow testing page.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for the older streets too, where a stiff shutoff valve or a corroded fitting can turn a small leak into a flooded floor overnight.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in San Juan Capistrano

Older San Juan Capistrano homes can have valves that have not moved in decades. Go gently with these four steps and do not force anything that will not turn.

1

Close the main valve

Turn the shutoff slowly. A valve that has sat for years can seize or snap, so stop if it resists and tell dispatch. The valve in the meter box belongs to the water provider, so do not force that one.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off the breaker for any room where water reaches outlets, a water heater or a garage freezer. If you must cross wet floor to reach the panel, wait for the plumber instead.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs and belongings out of the water's path. Sweep standing water toward a door or floor drain only if you can do it safely.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call and say whether the home is older, whether the water is coming from inside or the street side, and what the meter is doing.

Seasonal Risk

Winter Storms, Creeks and Drainage

San Juan Capistrano sits where San Juan Creek and Trabuco Creek run through town. Heavy winter rain raises groundwater and loads sewers, and a lateral with root damage is the first place a surge shows up as a backup.

Before the rainy season, a camera look at an older lateral is cheaper than a mid-storm emergency. After a storm, water damage restoration handles the cleanup if water reached the house. The same crew works the neighboring streets of Ladera Ranch and Laguna Niguel when a storm hits several towns at once.

Before You Call From an Older San Juan Capistrano Home

  • Check your water bill for the agency name, since the city's utility is moving to Santa Margarita Water District.
  • Locate the sewer cleanout, often a capped pipe outside near the house, and clear the area around it.
  • Note whether the house is in or near the Los Rios Historic District, since city review may apply to exterior work.
  • Write down when backups happened, since a pattern helps the plumber decide on a camera run.
