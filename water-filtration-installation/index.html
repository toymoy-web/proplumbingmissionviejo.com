Water Filtration Installation in Mission Viejo

Water filtration installation adds a treatment system to your plumbing to improve taste, smell or clarity, or to reduce specific contaminants. Systems range from a small under-sink unit for drinking water to a whole-home tank that filters every tap.

Water here is supplied by Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District, and each publishes an annual water quality report. The water is treated and disinfected to meet drinking water standards, so filtration is mostly about taste, chlorine and personal preference.

Start Here

I Want to Know What Is in My Water Before I Buy a System

That is the right order. Start with your district's annual water quality report, which lists disinfectants, minerals and regulated contaminants. Then decide what you want to change: taste, odor, hardness or something specific.

A common issue is the disinfectant itself. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 report states that its imported and locally produced water is treated with chloramines, a combination of chlorine and ammonia. Standard carbon filters handle chloramine poorly, and catalytic carbon is the usual match. Moulton Niguel and El Toro publish their own reports, so check yours.

If you want a full lab analysis for a specific concern, a certified lab can test a sample, and the plumber can then size a system to the result.

What to Ask Before You Buy a Filter

  • Is the product certified to NSF/ANSI standards for the contaminants you care about, such as 42, 53 or 58?
  • How often does the cartridge need changing, and what does it cost per year?
  • Does a whole-home unit reduce water pressure at upstairs showers?
  • Where will the drain line go for an RO unit, and does it need an air gap?
Code and Setup

How the Install Is Done to Code

Filter installs connect to the potable water line, so the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), applies. That includes shutoff valves, bypass, correct fittings and a drain with an air gap for RO units.

A whole-home system needs space near the main line and a bypass so you can service it without shutting off water to the house. Under-sink RO needs a drain connection, a storage tank and a faucet hole.

If your home has hard water as well as chlorine, a softener and a carbon filter often work together. See water softener installation for that side.

Related Services

Filtration and Your Other Plumbing

Filters change flow slightly, so a plumber often checks pressure at the same visit. If pressure is already low, leak detection rules out a hidden leak before adding a restriction. A new filter faucet also pairs with faucet and sink repair in the same trip.
