Hard Water

Water Softener Installation and Repair in Mission Viejo

A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium that make water hard. A salt-based unit does it by ion exchange, swapping those minerals for sodium or potassium and flushing the resin bed with brine on a schedule. The result is water that does not leave scale on fixtures, heating elements or glass.

Mission Viejo's water comes from Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District. Supply here is largely imported. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 water quality report, which covers 2025 data for Mission Viejo, lists average hardness of 15 grains per gallon, with readings from 12.3 to 17.5 across its system. That is why scale shows up on faucets, showerheads and water heaters across the city.

Start Here

My Softener Is Not Working Right and I Want the Whole System Checked

People call about softeners for two reasons: the water has turned hard again, or the system is noisy, wet around the base or using salt too fast. Both have fixable causes, and a plumber should check the whole system before quoting a replacement.

A salt bridge is a hard crust that forms in the brine tank and keeps salt from dissolving. A fouled resin bed no longer exchanges minerals. A stuck control valve or failed injector disrupts regeneration. A clogged drain line can flood the floor.

A service visit tests the hardness of the water coming out, inspects each of these parts, and tells you which repair is worth making and which means it is time for a new unit.

Local Rule

Check the Rules Before Installing a Salt-Based Softener

California law lets local agencies restrict self-regenerating water softeners because their brine adds salt to wastewater, which matters for recycled water. Mission Viejo irrigates over 500 acres of parks and slopes with reclaimed water, according to the City of Mission Viejo.

Rules vary by agency and change over time. Ask your district what applies before you buy, and the plumber can tell you about alternatives such as salt-free conditioners or a service-exchange tank that is regenerated off site.

Any softener install must meet the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including a proper drain connection with an air gap and a bypass valve.

Also Common

Softener Valves, Resin and Brine Well Service

Service calls for softeners are often about the control valve, the resin bed or the brine well. A unit that does not regenerate, a salt tank that bridges or a valve that leaks at the head can be repaired or the head replaced.

Tell dispatch the brand, the age and any error code, and whether you hear the unit trying to cycle.
