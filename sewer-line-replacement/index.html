Sewer Line Replacement: When Repair Is Not Enough

Sewer line replacement means installing a new lateral from the house to the public sewer main, in place of the old pipe. It is a different job from sewer line repair, which fixes one section. Replacement is the right call when the damage runs along most of the line.

Mission Viejo's oldest laterals date to the first tracts sold in 1966. After roughly six decades in the ground, some have root-fractured joints, bellies that hold water, and sections that have shifted or collapsed. When the camera shows that pattern, patching one spot leaves the next failure only a few feet away.

Methods

Trenchless or Open Trench

Trenchless replacement, usually pipe bursting, pulls a new pipe through the old path while the old pipe breaks apart underground. It needs small pits at each end instead of a trench along the whole run, which protects driveways, patios and mature landscaping.

Open trench replacement digs the full length, removes the old pipe and lays the new one. It is the method of choice when the old pipe has collapsed, when the grade must be corrected, or when the line is short and the surface is easy to restore.

Neither method is better in every case. The camera footage, the depth and what sits above the pipe decide it, and a good quote explains why one was chosen.

Local Context

Permits, Trees and Districts in Mission Viejo

The lateral runs under private property and then under the street or sidewalk to the main. A new lateral must meet the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Work in the public right-of-way normally needs a city permit, and the connection to the main follows your district's rules. Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District each serve parts of the city.

Mature trees line many Mission Viejo streets, and trenchless methods reduce the digging near their roots. A plumber should mark the line, check depth and discuss tree protection before anyone starts.

Some homeowners schedule a camera inspection before buying or selling, which can show a replacement is coming before it becomes an emergency.
