Tankless Water Heater Service in Mission Viejo

Tankless water heater service covers the maintenance and repair of an on-demand unit, including descaling the heat exchanger, cleaning inlet filters, checking venting and gas supply, and replacing failed parts. Because a tankless heater has no storage tank, every minute of hot water depends on the burner, sensors and flow working together.

A tankless unit often runs for years without attention, then starts throwing an error code or delivering lukewarm water. A service visit should start with diagnosis and an honest answer on whether the unit can be saved, not with a replacement quote.

Start Here

My Tankless Heater Shows an Error and the Water Is Cold

Most tankless units display a code when something is wrong. Write it down, along with the brand and model number on the label, before you reset anything. Codes tied to ignition, flame, exhaust or scale each point to a different part of the unit.

Scale is the most common hidden cause. Minerals from hard water coat the inside of the heat exchanger, restrict flow and make the unit overheat or shut off. A flush removes that scale, and in many cases the heater returns to normal with no part replaced.

If the flush does not fix it, the problem moves to sensors, the igniter, the fan, or the gas supply. These are repairs, and the plumber tells you which ones are worth doing on a unit of your age before replacing anything.

The Process

What a Tankless Service Visit Includes

  1. 1

    Check

    Error history, venting, gas supply and water pressure are inspected.

  2. 2

    Isolate

    Service valves are closed and the unit is connected to a flush pump.

  3. 3

    Flush

    A descaling solution circulates through the heat exchanger and the filters are cleaned.

  4. 4

    Test

    The unit is restarted and run at several flow rates to check temperature and codes.

Local Water

Why Hard Water Makes Tankless Service a Yearly Job

Mission Viejo is supplied by Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District, and the water they deliver is imported and mineral-rich. A tankless heater runs water through narrow passages at high temperature, which is where scale forms fastest.

Venting and gas piping for these units follow the manufacturer's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Most manufacturers recommend flushing at least once a year in hard water areas. The flush needs isolation valves on the hot and cold lines. If your unit was installed without them, adding the valves is a short job that makes every future service easier.

Scale also shortens the life of a tank unit. A tankless unit also draws more gas than a tank, so an undersized line is a common cause of poor performance, covered under gas line service. If you are comparing the two types, the water heater replacement page sets them side by side.

Related Help

When to Repair, Replace, or Add a Softener

A tankless heater under about 10 years old with one failed part is usually worth repairing. A unit with repeated failures and heavy scale may be better replaced, and a water softener would then protect the new one from the same problem.

If the heater has started to leak from a fitting or valve, water heater repair covers that. We also service tankless units in nearby Rancho Santa Margarita.
