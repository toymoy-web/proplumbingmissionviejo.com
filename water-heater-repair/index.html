No Hot Water? Emergency Water Heater Repair in Mission Viejo

Water heater repair is the diagnosis and replacement of a failed part, such as a thermostat, heating element, gas control valve, thermocouple or relief valve, so the existing tank keeps working. A heater that stops at midnight is an emergency for a family with kids, and the 24/7 line is built for that call.

Gas and electric heaters fail in different ways. A gas unit usually loses its pilot flame or thermocouple, while an electric unit loses an element or a thermostat. Telling the phone team which type you have, and what the tank is doing, speeds up the visit.

Before You Call: Quick Safety Steps

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or SoCalGas at 800-427-2200 from outside.
  • If water is pooling, close the cold supply valve above the heater.
  • For an electric unit, switch off its breaker before anyone opens a panel.
  • For a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to pilot or off.
  • Never plug or cap the relief valve discharge pipe, even if it drips.

Repair or Replace

When a Leak Means Replace, Not Repair

Where the water comes from decides the answer. A drip at a supply fitting or a drain valve is a small repair. Water seeping from the tank's bottom seam is a tank that has failed, and the right move is a water heater replacement before the leak grows.

Many Mission Viejo heaters sit in the garage, where a slow leak can run along the slab toward the house foundation and go unnoticed for weeks. If water appears at the slab edge and the heater is dry, the leak may be elsewhere, and leak detection will trace it.

A good rule of thumb: repair a unit under about eight years old if the part is cheap, and price a replacement for anything past 10 years. The plumber can tell you which side of that line your heater is on.

Gas Heaters

Pilot, Thermocouple and Gas Control Problems

On a gas heater, a pilot that will not stay lit usually points to a worn thermocouple or a dirty pilot tube. A heater that lights and then shuts off after a minute often has a failing flame sensor or a venting problem. Both are repairs rather than replacements on a heater under about 10 years old.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside before you call a plumber. Do not relight a pilot in a gas-smelling room. A leak at the gas control valve or a corroded gas connector is a safety repair that waits for nobody.

Electric Heaters

Elements, Thermostats and Tripped Breakers

Electric heaters have two elements and two thermostats. A lukewarm tank usually means the lower element has failed, while no hot water at all points to the upper element, a tripped high-limit reset or a breaker. Switch the breaker off before anyone opens an access panel.

Elements and thermostats are inexpensive parts, but a tank that has sat in hard water often has scale coating the element, which shortens its life. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 15 grains per gallon, so scale is a normal finding here. Ask the plumber to leave the old element on the floor so you can see the buildup yourself.

Keeping It Running

Maintenance That Prevents the Next Call

Flush a few gallons from the drain valve once a year to move sediment out before it hardens. Have the anode rod checked every few years, since it is the part that sacrifices itself to protect the tank. Set the thermostat near 120 degrees Fahrenheit, which the US Department of Energy suggests for most homes.

A water softener slows scale on the next heater, and a heater past 10 to 12 years old with a leak at the base is a candidate for replacement instead.

A common rule of thumb is to stop repairing when the repair costs about half the price of a new heater and the unit is past 8 to 10 years old. A leak from the tank itself cannot be repaired at any age. Ask for both numbers on the same visit so the choice is yours.

Local Water

Hard Water, Sediment and Heaters in Mission Viejo

Imported water supplied by Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District carries dissolved minerals. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 15 grains per gallon. Those minerals settle as scale on the tank floor and on elements, which causes rumbling noises and burned-out elements.

A yearly tank flush clears most of that sediment. Owners of tankless units face the same scale inside the heat exchanger, which is why tankless water heater service includes a descaling flush. The same crew repairs heaters in nearby Laguna Hills.
