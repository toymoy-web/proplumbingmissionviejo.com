The Neighborhood

Plumbing Help in Painted Trails

Painted Trails is a planned community at the northeast end of Mission Viejo, California, near the Trabuco Canyon area. If you live here, you can call (949) 325-3122 and give your street for a leak, a drain problem or a water heater issue.

Painted Trails sits close to other communities with their own city names, so addresses can be confusing. Some real estate sites file Painted Trails streets under neighboring places. This page explains how to give dispatch a clear address and what to know about the mix of homes and townhomes here.

Where It Is

Where Painted Trails Sits in Mission Viejo

The Lake Mission Viejo Association lists Painted Trails as District 83, with 486 homes. A local blog describes it as a master planned community on the north east end of Mission Viejo, directly across the street from Portola Hills, and notes that two homes it featured overlook Oso Reservoir.

Portola Hills is outside Mission Viejo and has its own plumbing service area, so the neighboring name is a reminder to give your street and ZIP and not just a community name. Public listings for Painted Trails streets show ZIP 92692, though some listing sites attach other place names to the same streets.

A local neighborhood guide describes the housing as a mix of single-family homes and townhomes developed in the early 2000s. That is one source, and we cannot confirm exact build years for each tract.

Plumbing Notes

Homes and Townhomes: What Changes for Plumbing

This section is general plumbing information, not a finding about any one Painted Trails home.

In a townhome, a leak may involve a neighbor. Walls, floors and ceilings are shared, and the building shutoff or the association may control who can turn water off. Know where your unit's shutoff is, and ask the association who handles shared lines, so you are not searching when water is running.

In a single-family home, the shutoff is yours, usually near the front of the house or in the meter box at the curb. The first step in any leak is to close it and watch whether the meter keeps moving with every fixture off.

Newer construction does not mean trouble-free. Water heater valves, toilet fill valves, supply lines and disposals still wear out on schedule, and hard water affects fixtures everywhere in the city. Hardness depends on which district serves your street, so check your bill before choosing treatment.

Before You Call From Painted Trails

  • Give dispatch your street, your ZIP and whether you live in a home or a townhome.
  • Locate your unit's shutoff or your main valve before there is a problem.
  • If you share walls, tell your neighbor and the association when water is running.
  • Find the water district name on your bill.
  • Read the date on the water heater's data plate.
