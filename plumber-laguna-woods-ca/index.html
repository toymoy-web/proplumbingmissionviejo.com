The City

Plumbing in a Small City Built Around a Retirement Village

Laguna Woods became Orange County's 32nd city on March 24, 1999. It covers 3.34 square miles and had 17,644 residents in the 2020 census. About 90 percent of the city is Laguna Woods Village, a private gated retirement community formerly known as Leisure World, and the rest holds businesses, homes and city hall.

Developer Ross Cortese started the community in 1962, and the first residents arrived in 1964. That makes many Village homes about 60 years old, so plumbing calls here look more like the oldest Mission Viejo tracts than a 1990s subdivision. Aging supply lines, worn shutoff valves, slow drains and water heaters in tight closets are the typical jobs.

The Village is also a place where the building matters as much as the pipe. Many homes are condo-style manors that share walls, risers and drain stacks, and a leak in one unit can reach a neighbor. That is why dispatch asks for the building and unit, not only the street.

Older Manors

What a 1960s and 1970s Home Needs From Its Plumber

Original copper and galvanized pieces are common in homes this age, and a valve that has not moved in decades can snap when forced. We work slowly at shutoffs, and we price any repiping separately after a pressure test, as laid out on our repiping page. Any replacement piping follows the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the mutual may add its own approval steps on top.

Laguna Woods water comes from El Toro Water District. The district's side ends at the meter, and everything past it is the owner's or the association's responsibility. Which part belongs to whom depends on whether the line serves one manor or several, so ask your mutual before a repair that touches a shared line.

Toilets, tubs and showers in older manors are often replaced for accessibility. Walk-in showers and comfort-height toilets are covered on our shower and bathtub page and our toilet page.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for the Village too, where a leak that starts in one manor at night can reach a neighbor before morning.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Laguna Woods

In a condo-style manor the right valve depends on the building, and a gentle hand matters on valves that are 60 years old. Take these four steps first.

1

Close the main valve

Look in the utility closet or water heater closet for your unit's shutoff and turn it slowly. If it will not move, stop and tell dispatch, since the building riser belongs to the mutual.

2

Switch off wet circuits

Switch off the breaker for any room where water is near outlets or the water heater. If the panel is in a wet closet, stay clear and say so on the call.

3

Clear the area

Lift rugs and belongings off wet floors and open the doors under the leaking fixture. Warn the neighbor below if water may be reaching the ceiling.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call with your mutual, building number, unit and the gate or visitor pass details. Say whether water is reaching a neighbor or a shared wall.
