Common Calls

Laguna Hills Plumbing Problems We See Most

Laguna Hills is a compact city of 6.6 square miles that incorporated on December 20, 1991, after 86 percent of voters approved cityhood in March of that year. Much of its housing and commercial space was built before incorporation, so the plumbing is already decades old.

Three problems come up again and again in a city this size and age: water heaters that have reached the end of their life, drains clogged by roots or grease, and supply-line leaks in older copper. Each is a different call, and each has a different fastest fix.

A tank past 10 years that starts leaking at the base is a replacement, not a repair. A drain that backs up in several fixtures at once is a main line problem. A meter that moves with everything off is a leak to trace before a bill grows.

Symptoms

Quick Guide to the Three Biggest Calls

No hot water or a leaking tank

Check the age. Past 10 years and leaking means plan a replacement tonight or tomorrow.

Several drains slow together

The shared line is blocked. Stop using water and book a main line clearing and camera look.

A meter that keeps turning

Water is escaping somewhere. Close the main shutoff and book leak detection.

A stain on a ceiling or wall

A supply or drain leak above. Turn off water to that area and call.

Local Water

Two Water Districts, One Small City

Both El Toro Water District and Moulton Niguel Water District list Laguna Hills in their service areas, so which one bills your home depends on the street. El Toro Water District also serves the neighboring city of Laguna Woods in full, so a block's address and ZIP may not tell you.

Check your bill, because the district named on it handles meter reads, high-bill questions and pressure complaints. Water heater swaps in either district fall under the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and need a permit and strapping. The imported supply carries dissolved minerals, which shows up as scale and short heater life.

Condos and townhomes add one more wrinkle. A leak in one unit can reach a neighbor, so water shutoff, access and who pays are usually set by the association. Tell the management office early.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock whichever district bills you, and the first call is the same either way.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Laguna Hills

Laguna Hills has many condos, townhomes and small commercial units, so the right valve depends on the building. Work through these four steps while you wait.

1

Close the main valve

In a house, look at the front wall or the garage. In a condo or townhome, check the utility closet or water heater closet for a unit valve, and call the property manager for the building shutoff.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Switch off the breaker for any area where water is near outlets or appliances. In a condo, warn the neighbor below if water may be coming through the ceiling.

3

Clear the area

Move belongings off the floor and clear a path from the entry to the leak. Keep the hallway or walkway open so the plumber can carry tools in.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call with the building name, your unit number and the nearest cross street, such as El Toro Road or Moulton Parkway. Say whether the leak is reaching a neighbor or a shared wall.

Before You Call From a Laguna Hills Condo or Business

  • Give the unit number, the building name and a contact who can let the plumber in.

  • Photograph the damage, note the time it started and who authorized the call.
