Faucet and Sink Repair in Mission Viejo

Faucet and sink repair covers the visible fixture and everything beneath it: the cartridge or valve inside the faucet, the supply lines and shutoff valves, the trap and drain, and the seals around the sink. Most calls start with a drip or a puddle in the cabinet.

A drip is worth fixing quickly. The EPA reports that a faucet dripping once per second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year, and that shows up on your district water bill.

Symptoms

What the Problem Usually Is

Drip from the spout

A worn cartridge, seat or washer inside the faucet body.

Leak at the handle or base

A failing O-ring or loose packing around the handle.

Weak or sputtering flow

Scale in the aerator or a partly closed shutoff valve. Hard water causes the first.

Water under the sink

A loose slip nut on the trap, a cracked supply line, or a failing valve.

Local Water

How Mission Viejo's Water Affects Your Fixtures

The Santa Margarita, Moulton Niguel and El Toro water districts supply imported water that carries dissolved minerals. Those minerals build up inside aerators, cartridges and showerheads, which is why many faucets in the city lose pressure well before they actually fail.

A vinegar soak removes light scale from an aerator. Heavy buildup inside a cartridge is a repair job. If scale keeps returning across the house, a water softener is the long-term fix.

The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), works alongside state efficiency rules. California limits bathroom faucets to 1.2 gallons per minute and kitchen faucets to 1.8, so a replacement should carry a compliant aerator. Check the box before you buy.

Quick Checks Before You Call

  • Look under the sink with a flashlight and dry any wet spots to see where the water returns.
  • Close the two supply valves under the sink to stop a spraying leak.
  • Unscrew the aerator and check it for white or gray scale.
  • Note the faucet brand if it is printed on the body, since it helps with parts.
