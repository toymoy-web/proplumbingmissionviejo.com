Leak Detection in Mission Viejo: Find It Before You Cut

Leak detection is the process of locating a plumbing leak without opening walls or floors at random, using listening equipment, thermal imaging, moisture meters and pressure tests. A leak you can see is easy. The ones that cost money are inside walls, under slabs or in the line between the meter and the house.

Most callers do not know where the water is going, only that something is wrong. This page starts with the symptom and explains how the cause is found, so you know what a visit involves before you call the 24/7 line.

Emergency Service

When a Leak Becomes an Emergency in Mission Viejo

A leak is an emergency when water is visibly spreading, a ceiling is sagging, the meter runs fast with everything off, or you cannot reach the source. Close the main shutoff first, then call the 24/7 line. A dispatcher asks where the water shows, whether it is wet or only damp, and how fast the meter moves.

Emergency leak detection starts with that call. We send a plumber with the tools to trace the leak without opening walls at random, and we quote the price before work. Many callers only need the leak found, so say so and we will plan the visit around locating it.

Start Here

My Water Bill Went Up and I Cannot Find Why

A rising bill with no change in habits is the most common reason people call. Check your meter before anything else. Turn off every fixture and appliance in the house, then watch the meter. Many meters have a small low-flow dial or triangle that turns when any water moves.

If it keeps moving, water is leaking somewhere between the meter and the fixtures. If it stays still, the usage may be inside the house, such as a toilet that runs only at night. A few drops of food coloring in each tank find those silent toilet leaks.

Your district can verify the meter read. Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District and El Toro Water District each handle readings and high-bill questions for their own customers, so start with the one named on your bill.

Symptoms

Signs of a Hidden Leak

Meter moves with everything off

The leak is on the pressurized supply side, in the house or the service line.

Warm spot on a floor

A hot water line under a slab may be leaking. See slab leak detection.

Sound of running water

Hissing or trickling in a quiet house is a strong clue that pressure is escaping from a pipe.

Stains, bubbles or mold

Damp drywall, peeling paint, a musty smell or a ceiling stain below a bathroom.

Local Context

Why Older Mission Viejo Plumbing Develops Leaks

The first Mission Viejo homes were sold in 1966, and Casta del Sol added 1,927 homes between 1972 and 1987. Copper supply lines of that age can develop pinhole leaks, often where water chemistry or flow has worn the pipe wall from the inside.

Homes in Palmia, built from 1988 to 1999, are newer, but their water heaters, valves and flexible supply lines are now at an age where small leaks begin. A leak under a sink or behind a washer is a quiet, steady cost.

The service line from the meter to the house is another place leaks start, and it is buried. A leak there often shows up as a soft patch in a yard or a meter that spins with every fixture off. See water line repair and replacement for that repair.

Outdoors

Leaks Outside the House Spin the Meter Too

Not every hidden leak is inside the walls. A leak on the line between the meter and the house, an irrigation valve stuck open or a hose bib dripping into a planter can all keep the meter moving. A patch of lawn that stays green in a dry week, a damp strip along the foundation or the sound of water at the meter with the house shut off all point outdoors.

A simple test separates the two. Turn the irrigation controller off, close the main valve at the house and read the meter again. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the buried line is the suspect, and the next step is water line repair. A swimming pool loses water differently, and pool leak detection uses its own tests.

Before the Visit

Keep a Simple Leak Log for the Plumber

Write down the meter reading at bedtime and again in the morning, with the date, while nothing in the house is running. Photograph any stain or damp spot with a coin or ruler beside it for scale, and note when you first saw it. A short log shows the plumber how fast the water is going and where to test first.

It also helps if a leak ends up involving an insurer. A dated record of when a problem was noticed and what was done next is easier to defend than a memory.

Caller Questions

Water Through a Ceiling or Wall

A stain on a ceiling or a damp patch on a wall is usually water from a supply line, a drain or a fixture above, and the stain is rarely under the source. We start at the room above, check the shower pan, tub drain and supply stops, then use moisture and thermal readings to follow the water back.

Tell dispatch which floor the stain is on and whether a bathroom sits above it. That saves time and keeps drywall cuts small.

Caller Questions

Irrigation Lines and Outdoor Leaks

A sprinkler valve that runs fast, a wet spot with no sprinkler nearby or a patch of lush grass above a line can point to a break in the irrigation supply. Closing the irrigation valve and watching the meter separates a yard leak from a house leak.

Tell us where the wet ground is and whether the meter still moves with the irrigation off. Backflow devices on irrigation are covered on our backflow testing page.

Caller Questions

Rentals, Multi-Unit Buildings and Homes for Sale

Owners of rentals, duplexes and small multi-unit buildings often call with a high bill and no visible water. We shut off one unit at a time at the meter and shutoffs to narrow the search before testing pipes.

A house in escrow can be tested on a short timeline too. Say that on the call so the visit is scheduled around the inspection window.

Also Common

Smaller Leak Questions

Spa and hot tub leaks near equipment are checked with a bucket test and pressure test, and a leak next to the meter box usually involves the fittings at the meter. A leak that returns after another plumber's repair is traced from the start, since the first fix may have hit the wrong spot.

Local Coverage

Where We Work Across Mission Viejo

Hidden leaks show up differently by neighborhood. In Casta del Sol and Palmia the first clue is often a spinning meter in a single-story home, while on the hillside streets of Pacific Hills the sign can be a wet slope or a soft patch at a retaining wall.

Mission Viejo was planned with roads in the valleys and houses on the hills, so a leak can travel downhill before it appears. That is why tracing starts at the meter, not at the wet spot. Slab-level cases move on to slab leak detection.
