Where It Is

Where Pacific Hills Sits in Mission Viejo

The Lake Mission Viejo Association lists two Pacific Hills districts on its public district page. District 72, Pacific Hills, has 602 homes, and District 75, Pacific Hills East, has 75. The same list has a separate District 73 called Pacific Knolls, with 511 homes. We treat Pacific Knolls as its own name and do not describe it here.

The City of Mission Viejo community profile includes Pacific Hills Park among the city's 33 neighborhood parks, and a local neighborhood guide places the 7.81-acre park at 28050 Fieldcrest. Local real estate guides describe Pacific Hills as a hillside area with mountain views. Public listings for Pacific Hills Drive show ZIP 92692.

Mission Viejo's master plan, drafted by Donald Bren according to Wikipedia, put roads in the valleys and houses on the hills. That is general history for the whole city, and it is the reason slope matters to plumbing here.

Plumbing Notes

What a Hillside Home Can Mean for Plumbing

This section is general plumbing information for sloped properties, not a finding about any one house in Pacific Hills.

Water pressure rises as the elevation of a home drops below its supply. A rule of thumb is about 0.43 psi for every foot of height. The California Plumbing Code calls for a pressure regulator when supply pressure exceeds 80 psi. A gauge screwed onto an outdoor spigot shows your number in a minute, and a reading near or above 80 is worth having checked, because high pressure wears out fixtures, supply lines and water heater valves early.

Slope also changes where water goes. A leak at a hillside home can run downhill along the foundation or into a neighbor's yard before it shows up as a wet spot, so the reliable first move is to shut the main valve. A flat meter reading test, with every fixture off, tells you whether water is moving at all.

Drain lines on a grade usually flow fast, which helps, but a belly or a root intrusion still causes backups. If the same drain stops twice, a camera look shows the cause.

Before You Call From Pacific Hills

  • Give dispatch your street and cross street, since several Mission Viejo areas share the 92692 code.
  • Shut the main water valve if water is moving and you cannot see why.
  • Screw a pressure gauge onto an outdoor spigot if you have one and note the reading.
  • Find the water district name on your bill, since the city is served by three.
  • Note where the water heater sits, in a garage or a closet, before you call.
