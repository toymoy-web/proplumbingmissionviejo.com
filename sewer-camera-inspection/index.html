Sewer Camera Inspection in Mission Viejo

A sewer camera inspection is a visual survey of the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public main. A plumber feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the cleanout, and a screen shows the pipe wall as the camera moves. A transmitter on the camera head lets a locator on the surface mark the path and depth.

It answers the questions a cable cannot. Is the pipe cracked or offset, are roots growing in at the joints, is there a low spot that holds water, or is the pipe sound and the clog just grease? Knowing that before spending money on a repair avoids guessing.

When to Book

Situations Where a Camera Run Pays for Itself

Buying or selling a home

A sewer line is hidden, expensive to replace and rarely checked in a standard home inspection.

Repeat clogs

If a drain clogs again within months, the camera shows why.

Before a repair or remodel

A camera confirms scope, so quotes are based on footage, not guesses.

Older lines and mature trees

Roots from nearby trees find joints in aging pipe.

The Process

How an Inspection Goes

  1. 1

    Access

    The plumber finds the cleanout, or pulls a toilet or uses a vent if there is none.

  2. 2

    Run

    The camera is pushed through the line to the main, recording as it goes.

  3. 3

    Locate

    The transmitter lets a locator mark the path and depth on the ground.

  4. 4

    Report

    You get the footage, the findings and repair options with prices if needed.

Reading the Report

What a Good Inspection Report Includes

A useful report shows footage with distances, so you can find each defect later. Many professionals grade defects with a standard such as the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program coding, which rates cracks, roots and breaks consistently. Ask which method the plumber uses.

The camera has limits. It cannot pass a full blockage, so the line may need clearing first. A flowing line hides some bellies, and a clean video is a snapshot of the day it was taken.

Sewer work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The district that serves your street, Santa Margarita Water District, Moulton Niguel Water District or El Toro Water District, owns the main, and the footage shows which side of the connection any damage sits on.
