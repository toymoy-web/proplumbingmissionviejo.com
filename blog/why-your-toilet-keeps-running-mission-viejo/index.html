Why Your Toilet Keeps Running: Mission Viejo Water Facts

A running toilet is usually a cheap fix. Finding the cause takes ten minutes and a few drops of food coloring.

By the Mission Viejo Pro Plumbing team · Published October 2, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • The EPA says a toilet that runs constantly can waste 200 gallons of water or more a day.
  • A worn flapper is the most common cause, and it costs only a few dollars to replace.
  • A silent leak does not make noise, so use a dye test to find it.
  • California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush, so an old toilet is a double drain on the water bill.
The Cost

What a Running Toilet Costs You

The EPA's WaterSense program says that a toilet running constantly could waste 200 gallons of water or more every day, and that the cause is most often an old, faulty flapper. Over a year, that is tens of thousands of gallons. EPA also says the average household loses nearly 10,000 gallons a year to leaks of all kinds.

In Mission Viejo, that water arrives from Santa Margarita, Moulton Niguel or El Toro Water District, and you pay for each unit that passes through the meter. A running toilet is one of the easiest leaks to fix and one of the costliest to ignore.

The good news is that most causes are small parts. The guide below walks through the five usual ones, from the cheapest fix to the one that signals a bigger job.

Find the Cause: A Ten-Minute Dye Test

  • Take the tank lid off and set it somewhere safe.
  • Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank water. Do not flush.
  • Wait about 10 minutes and look at the bowl.
  • If color appears in the bowl, water is leaking past the flapper or flush valve.
  • Flush right away to clear the dye, so it does not stain the tank.

Fixing It Yourself

How to Replace a Flapper

Close the small valve behind the toilet, flush to empty the tank and unhook the old flapper from its chain and the pegs on the overflow tube. Take it to a hardware store so the new one matches. Hook the new flapper on, adjust the chain to leave slight slack and open the valve.

Run the dye test again to make sure it seals. If the toilet is more than a decade old, replacing the whole flush valve assembly is worth considering, since the seat itself may be rough or worn.

Southern California tap water is treated with chloramine, which water industry research has linked to faster aging of some rubber parts. If flappers fail every couple of years, a chloramine-resistant flapper is a sensible choice.

Why Replace

When the Toilet Itself Is the Problem

California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush. A toilet made before the 1994 federal standard can use 3.5 gallons or more per flush, nearly three times as much. The EPA estimates that a family of four replacing older toilets with WaterSense labeled models could save more than 16,000 gallons a year.

Mission Viejo's first homes date to 1966, and some older houses may still have original or early replacement toilets. If yours is among them and the flush valve is also worn, replacement can pay for itself in lower water bills. Local water districts have offered rebates on high-efficiency toilets at times, so ask yours before you buy.

Replacement also pairs with other bathroom work. If you are planning a remodel, our shower and bathtub installation page covers the plumbing behind the walls.

When to Call

Signs It Is Not Just a Toilet Problem

If the toilet is quiet and the meter still moves with every fixture off, the leak is elsewhere. Run the two-hour meter test, and if it shows a leak, leak detection finds the source. A toilet that gurgles when another drain runs points to the sewer line, which is a job for drain cleaning.

Call a plumber if the tank cracks, the base leaks, the floor feels soft or the supply line is corroded. Water at the base of the toilet is a seal or flange problem that does not fix itself.

Common Mistakes

Mistakes That Turn a Cheap Fix Into a Costly One

Overtightening is the classic. Tank bolts and supply nuts need snug, not cranked, and a cracked porcelain tank or bowl cannot be repaired. Use a wrench gently, and stop when the connection holds.

The wrong flapper is the next most common. Many toilets take a 2-inch flapper and some take a 3-inch, and a mismatched part never seals. Bring the old flapper to the store, or check the brand and model printed in the tank.

Skipping the dye test is a quieter mistake. If you replace the flapper and skip the test, you will not know whether the leak is fixed until the next bill arrives. A tangled chain, or a chain that is too short, can also keep the flapper from closing, so check it before you blame the part.

Mission Viejo Note

Mission Viejo Water and Toilet Parts

Santa Margarita Water District reports an average hardness of 15 grains per gallon, and mineral scale can build on fill valves and flappers over time. A part that sticks may be scale and not wear, so look at it before you replace it.

A running toilet can show up on a bill, so ask your utility whether it offers usage alerts. We handle toilet calls across Laguna Niguel and Ladera Ranch.

FAQ

Running Toilet Questions

The EPA says a toilet that runs constantly can waste 200 gallons or more a day. A slow, silent leak wastes less but still adds up over a year.

A flapper that leaks slightly lets water drain from the tank, so the fill valve turns on to refill it. That cycle is sometimes called a phantom flush.

A flapper or fill valve is a low-cost part. A new flush valve or toilet costs more, and a plumber can give you the price before work starts.

If it predates 1994, it likely uses much more water per flush than a 1.28-gallon model. The water savings can justify the change.
