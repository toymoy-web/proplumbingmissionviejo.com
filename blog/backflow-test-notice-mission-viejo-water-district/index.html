Backflow Test Notice From SMWD: What Mission Viejo Owners Do

A letter from your water district about backflow is routine. Here is what it asks for and the steps to close it out.

By the Mission Viejo Pro Plumbing team · Published October 2, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • A backflow notice means your property has a registered assembly that needs its annual test and a report filed with your district.
  • Santa Margarita Water District's cross-connection control plan took effect on July 1, 2025, under the state's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook.
  • Use a tester your district accepts, and ask for the certification number before the visit.
  • Missing the deadline can bring follow-up notices and, in some cases, service shutoff.
The Notice

What a Backflow Testing Notice Actually Means

Backflow is the reversal of water flow, which can pull used or contaminated water back into the drinking supply. A backflow assembly is a valve device that prevents it. Lawn irrigation systems, fire sprinkler lines, commercial kitchens and properties that use recycled water commonly have one.

Your district tracks every registered assembly and mails a notice each year before the test is due. The letter lists the device and its location, gives a deadline, and tells you to hire a certified tester and have the result sent to the district.

The notice is not a fine and it is not an accusation. It is a routine compliance step, like a car registration renewal, and handling it early keeps it routine.

The Rules

Where the Requirement Comes From

California requires public water systems to run cross-connection control programs. Santa Margarita Water District's 2026 water quality report states that its Cross-Connection Control Management Plan took effect on July 1, 2025. The plan follows the State Water Resources Control Board's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook, which replaced the earlier Title 17 sections that used to govern the topic.

In practical terms, that means your district writes the schedule and the paperwork rules for its customers. A deadline from Santa Margarita can differ from one from Moulton Niguel or El Toro, so follow the notice you received. The California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), covers where assemblies must be installed.

Steps to Close Out Your Notice

  • Read the notice and note the device location, the account number and the deadline.
  • Hire a tester your district accepts. Ask for the tester's certification number and confirm it with the district.
  • Clear shrubs, mulch and stored items so the tester can reach the assembly and its test ports.
  • Expect a short water shutoff to the irrigation line or building during the test.
  • Make sure the passing report is filed with your district, and keep a copy.

Local Context

Why Mission Viejo Takes This Seriously

Mission Viejo irrigates more than 500 acres of parks, medians and slopes with reclaimed water, according to the City of Mission Viejo. Recycled and drinking water lines must stay separate, and backflow assemblies are part of how districts prove they do.

Businesses also feel it. Restaurants, medical offices and retail centers often carry several assemblies, and a property manager may need all of them tested on one schedule. That is the kind of job our commercial plumbing team handles.

Next Steps

If the Device Fails or You Missed the Deadline

A failed test is not a crisis. The tester repairs the device, usually with new seals or springs, and retests it, then files the passing result. If the assembly is old or damaged, replacing it can cost less than repeated repairs. Our backflow testing page explains the repair and replacement options.

If you missed the deadline, book the test now and call your district to tell them. The same 24/7 team tests devices in nearby Rancho Santa Margarita, where many properties have irrigation assemblies.

Questions to Ask a Backflow Tester Before You Book

  • What is your certification number, and does my district accept it?
  • Does the quote include the district's report form and any filing fee?
  • What happens if the device fails, and how much do common repair parts cost?
  • Can you test every assembly on my property in one visit?
  • How long will the water be off, and which fixtures does it affect?
HOAs and Managers

Who Is Responsible in an Association or a Rented Property

The notice goes to the account holder for the meter that the device protects. In a gated community, that is often the association, which manages common-area irrigation and may handle the test for you. If you got a letter for a device on your own lot, the responsibility is yours.

Landlords and property managers should read the lease to see who handles testing. Many leases put compliance on the owner, since the device is part of the building's plumbing, and a tenant who finds a notice should forward it to management at once.

Keep each year's report together with the device's serial number. When a district asks for history, or a buyer's inspector asks about irrigation, a folder of passing reports answers the question in a minute.

Timing

When to Book the Test

Book as soon as the notice arrives instead of waiting for the deadline. Testers get busy near due dates, and a failed test needs time for parts and a retest. Early booking also leaves room to fix a damaged device before the district follows up.

New irrigation systems and remodels are another trigger. A device installed or moved during a landscape project needs its own first test and registration, and the installer should hand you the paperwork. If a notice arrives for a device you do not recognize, call the district before you ignore it.

Records

Keep a Simple Backflow File

Create one folder, paper or digital, for each assembly on the property. Put the district notice, the tester's report, the device's serial number and the date of each repair in it. A buyer's inspector, an insurer or a new property manager can then see the history in a minute.

Mission Viejo Note

What Santa Margarita Water District Requires

Santa Margarita Water District states that all backflow assemblies must be tested and that testing is the responsibility of the water account holder. The district points owners to the Orange County Health Care Agency list of certified testers, and it asks that leaking or malfunctioning devices be reported to backflow@smwd.com.

The rule says all assemblies, so include irrigation devices in your count. Owners in Rancho Santa Margarita and San Juan Capistrano receive similar notices from their own districts, so read the one in your hand.
