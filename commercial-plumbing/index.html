Commercial Plumbing for Mission Viejo Businesses

Commercial plumbing covers the water, drain and gas systems in buildings used for business: restaurants, offices, medical suites, retail units and multi-tenant properties. The principles match residential work, but the pipes are larger, usage is heavier and downtime costs money.

A restroom out of service or a floor drain backing up is a customer-facing problem. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, and the plumber who arrives expects to coordinate with a manager, a tenant or a landlord.

What to Do While You Wait for the Plumber

  • Close the nearest shutoff valve or the main, and keep customers and staff away from wet floors, which are a slip hazard.
  • Stop using the affected sinks, toilets and floor drains until they are cleared.
  • Move stock, paper records and electronics off the floor, and keep water away from outlets and panels.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time it started, since your carrier and your landlord will ask.
  • Tell the property manager or landlord, and write down who authorized the work.

Local Rules

Grease, Codes and Accessibility in Mission Viejo

Food service businesses in Orange County must control fats, oils and grease, which means properly sized traps or interceptors that are cleaned on schedule. Grease buildup is a leading cause of sewer blockages, so local sanitation programs inspect them.

Commercial plumbing falls under the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Public restrooms also need to meet accessibility rules for fixture height and clearance, which comes into play during tenant improvements.

Many commercial sites also have backflow assemblies that need annual testing, covered under backflow testing and prevention.

Food Service and Tenant Work

Restaurants, Tenant Improvements and New Suites

A restaurant build-out or a tenant improvement changes the plumbing the moment a wall moves. Hand sinks, mop sinks, three-compartment sinks, floor sinks and grease control all have placement and sizing rules, and the county health inspector checks them before a food business opens.

Plan the plumbing before the cabinets and equipment are ordered. Moving a floor drain after the slab is poured, or adding a restroom to a suite never designed for one, costs far more than getting the rough-in right the first time.

Mission Viejo has large retail centers, medical offices and many small suites, and the older buildings from the 1970s and 1980s may have cast iron drains and original risers. A camera inspection before a lease or a remodel shows what you are inheriting.
