The Community

Plumbing in a Gated Hillside Community in Wagon Wheel Canyon

Coto de Caza sits in Wagon Wheel Canyon in southeast Orange County and began as a 1968 hunting lodge concept before the master plan was approved in 1983. The community opened in 1986 with about 5,000 homes planned and was finished around 2003. It is an unincorporated, guard-gated community of roughly 4,000 homes run by a homeowners association, with 14,710 residents counted in 2020.

That age range shapes the plumbing calls. A house here is between about 23 and 40 years old, which is the window when the first water heater has been replaced, supply valves have stiffened and irrigation systems on larger lots show wear. It is too early for the 1960s root-fed laterals of older Mission Viejo tracts, so the calls skew toward heaters, valves, fixtures and landscape water.

The setting adds its own wrinkle. Thomas F. Riley Wilderness Park surrounds the community, and a 36-hole Robert Trent Jones Jr. golf course winds through it, so many lots back onto open land or fairway. Slopes, long runs and irrigation lines make leak tracing worth doing before anyone digs.

Water and Pressure

Water Service, Hills and What They Mean for Your Pipes

Santa Margarita Water District serves Coto de Caza, and the district's 2026 water quality report lists hardness averaging 15 grains per gallon. Scale builds inside tank heaters and on fixtures here, so a yearly flush and a look at the anode rod pay off. Our water softener page explains what the association and the district allow.

Houses in the canyon sit at different elevations, around 709 feet at the center of the CDP, and water pressure changes from street to street. A pressure regulator at the main shutoff protects fixtures and the water heater, and it is one of the first things we check on a service call. Any new regulator, heater or supply work follows the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), with a permit where the city or county requires one.

Large lots mean long irrigation runs, and backflow devices on those lines need an annual test. See backflow testing and prevention for how the district notice works.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, so a supply line that fails at 2 a.m. gets the same response as a weekday call, with the gate handled by phone.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Coto de Caza

Larger homes on sloped lots can move a lot of water before anyone notices. Take these four steps first, then tell dispatch how the gate works.

1

Close the main valve

The shutoff on a larger Coto de Caza home may be at a side yard or beside the garage, often near a pressure regulator. Turn the shutoff clockwise and leave the regulator untouched.

2

Cut power near the water

If water is near the garage panel, the heater or a spare refrigerator, switch off that breaker first. Do not wade through water to reach it.

3

Clear the area

Move vehicles and stored boxes off a wet floor, and turn the irrigation controller to off if the water is outdoors. Leave the driveway open for the plumber's truck.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Give your street, your cross street, the gate or guard house instructions and whether the lot backs onto a slope or open land.
