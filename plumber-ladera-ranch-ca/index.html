Seasons

Plumbing by Season in a Newer Master-Planned Community

Ladera Ranch is a 4,000-acre master-planned community that broke ground around 1999 and was substantially complete by 2006, with about 8,100 homes in a string of villages. Almost every house here is under 30 years old, which changes the pattern of plumbing calls compared with older neighbors like Mission Viejo.

In the dry months, irrigation is the main water user. The community uses central computer controls for irrigation, and a stuck valve, a broken head or a cracked line can run for days before anyone sees a puddle. A sudden jump in the bill in summer is the first clue.

In the rainy season, the concerns shift. Roofs, gutters and drains handle more water, and a slow drain or a gurgling toilet is more likely to show up when the ground is saturated. Winter is also when a water heater that has run for 10 or 15 years tends to be missed until it fails.

Newer Homes

What a Home Built After 1999 Needs From Its Plumber

Newer homes rarely have the corroded pipe or root-fed laterals of 1960s tracts, so the calls skew toward fixtures, water heaters, valves and irrigation. Tankless units are common in newer construction, and they need a yearly flush, covered on our tankless service page.

Santa Margarita Water District serves Ladera Ranch, and the community was designed around efficient irrigation and a biofiltration system for runoff. That design means backflow devices on irrigation lines are standard, and they need yearly tests. See backflow testing and prevention.

Builder warranties on a house under 10 years old may cover some plumbing defects. Before you pay for a repair, check the warranty and the date, since a covered defect is worth a call to the builder first.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, and a newer home is not exempt, since a failed supply hose, irrigation valve or water heater connection can flood a garage at any hour.

Emergency Prep

Before We Arrive: If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Ladera Ranch

Newer homes often have the main shutoff and a pressure regulator close together, and a hissing sound there is worth mentioning on the call. Take these four steps first.

1

Close the main valve

Look at the front or side of the house and in the garage. If a pressure regulator sits beside the shutoff, close the shutoff and leave the regulator alone.

2

Kill power to wet areas

Garages hold the panel, the water heater and often a second refrigerator. Turn off the breakers for any wet garage circuit before you step in.

3

Clear the area

Move cars, bins and stored boxes off a wet garage floor. Turn off the irrigation controller if the leak is in the yard.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Call and name your village and street, and say whether the home is behind a gate such as Covenant Hills.
